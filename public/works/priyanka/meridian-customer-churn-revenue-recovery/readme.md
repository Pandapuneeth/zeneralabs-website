# Customer Churn Prediction & Revenue-Recovery Analytics

**Client:** Meridian Retail Group (mid-size omni-channel fashion & lifestyle retailer)
**Engagement type:** Zenera Labs internal engineering capability assessment

---

## ⚠️ Synthetic Dataset Disclaimer

**No real Meridian Retail Group data was provided or used in this project.**
All data in `data/raw/` and `data/processed/` is synthetically generated by
`src/data_generation.py` using a fixed random seed (42) and does **not**
represent real customers, transactions, or behaviour of Meridian Retail
Group or any other real company. It was designed to have realistic
statistical structure (recency/frequency/monetary relationships,
support-driven risk, marketing-engagement effects, etc.) so the analytics
pipeline can be exercised in a defensible way, but any figures reported
from it should be understood as a **methodology demonstration**, not a
real business finding.

---

## 1. Business Context

Meridian Retail Group has seen a **14% drop in repeat purchase rate**
over the last two quarters despite stable traffic and marketing spend.
Leadership suspects **silent churn among mid-value customers** — customers
who aren't complaining or unsubscribing, they're just quietly buying less
and eventually not at all.

## 2. Problem Statement

Build a churn-prediction and revenue-recovery analytics capability that
identifies at-risk customers early enough for retention outreach to be
effective, while being explainable and auditable enough for the client's
internal team to trust and operate.

## 3. Objectives

1. Predict which customers are likely to stop purchasing within the next 60 days
2. Quantify how precise the model is for actionable retention outreach
3. Estimate how much revenue is at risk from predicted churners
4. Identify which retention intervention (Discount / Loyalty Nudge /
   Personal Outreach) is likely to provide the best ROI
5. Surface the major explainable churn drivers
6. Ensure the client's internal team can rerun the pipeline monthly

## 4. Dataset Description

Four connected, synthetic CSV files covering an **18-month observation
window (2024-03-01 to 2025-08-30)** for **6,000 synthetic customers**:

| File | Rows | Grain |
|---|---|---|
| `customers.csv` | 6,000 | 1 row per customer |
| `transactions.csv` | ~128,000 | 1 row per purchase |
| `marketing_touchpoints.csv` | ~153,000 | 1 row per marketing contact |
| `support_tickets.csv` | ~10,500 | 1 row per support ticket |

All child tables reference `customer_id` in `customers.csv`; referential
integrity is verified in `src/data_audit.py` (0 orphaned rows found).

Full column-level documentation is in
[`reports/data_audit/data_dictionary.md`](reports/data_audit/data_dictionary.md).

### How the synthetic data was designed

Each synthetic customer has a hidden ("latent," never written to disk)
engagement trajectory that drifts over the 18 months and probabilistically
drives their purchase frequency, spend, marketing responsiveness, and
support-ticket generation. Because this process is smooth, noisy, and
probabilistic — not a hard rule — realistic, *learnable but not trivially
encoded* correlations exist between behaviour and eventual churn, e.g.:

- Higher purchase frequency correlates with higher customer value
- Declining recent-vs-prior purchase frequency correlates with churn risk
- Poor support experiences (slow resolution, low satisfaction) correlate
  with reduced future engagement
- Low marketing engagement correlates with churn risk
- Discount-sensitive customers behave differently from full-price
  Premium Shoppers
- Online and in-store channel mix varies by customer segment

No column directly encodes churn, churn probability, or account status —
see Section 6, Leakage Prevention.

## 5. Churn Definition

**Full write-up:** [`reports/churn_definition/churn_definition.md`](reports/churn_definition/churn_definition.md)

- **Eligible population** (as of a `prediction_date`): customers with
  ≥1 transaction in the 60 days strictly before `prediction_date`
  (i.e., "currently active" customers — the only ones retention outreach
  is actually actionable for).
- **Target:** `churn = 1` if the customer has **zero** transactions in
  `(prediction_date, prediction_date + 60 days]`; else `churn = 0`.
- **Actual churn prevalence achieved:**
  - **9.56%** at the single most recent feasible snapshot (matches the
    brief's ~9% target almost exactly)
  - **11.8%** averaged across all 13 monthly sliding snapshots used for
    modeling (11.4% train / 12.0% validation / 12.6% test)

This was validated empirically, not hard-coded — see the churn-definition
report for the sensitivity analysis across different eligibility lookback
windows (60d / 90d / 120d).

## 6. Leakage Prevention

**Full audit:** [`reports/churn_definition/leakage_audit.csv`](reports/churn_definition/leakage_audit.csv)

Every feature in the modeling table is computed using **only** records
dated `<= prediction_date` (bounded further to a 180-day trailing window
for the rolling behavioural features). For every candidate feature we
asked: *"Would this information genuinely be available at the prediction
date?"* Features that could only be known using future information
(e.g., an unbounded "last order date" or a live "account status" flag)
are explicitly excluded and documented with a REMOVE action in the audit,
even though such columns were never included in the raw schema to begin
with — the audit exists as a defensive, explicit control, not just a
description of what happens to already-safe data.

## 7. Methodology

### 7.1 Time-based validation strategy

This is a time-series-adjacent problem: using a random train/test split
would leak future information across the split boundary (since features
and neighbouring snapshots share overlapping histories) and wouldn't
reflect how the model will actually be used — trained on the past,
deployed on the present. Instead:

- 13 monthly `prediction_date` snapshots are built across the observation
  window (`src/preprocessing.py`)
- Snapshots are sorted chronologically and split **~60% earliest → TRAIN,
  ~20% next → VALIDATION, ~20% most recent → TEST**
- No customer/date combination is shuffled across splits

### 7.2 RFM approach

Recency, Frequency, and Monetary features are computed per customer as of
each prediction date, bounded to historical data only:
`recency_days`, `frequency_180d`, `monetary_180d`, `avg_order_value`,
plus a `recent_vs_prior_freq_delta` trend feature. These are the raw
inputs from which RFM segments (Champions, Loyal, At-Risk, Dormant,
Mid-Value, etc.) will be derived in `notebooks/02_eda_segmentation.ipynb`
— segments are **not** hard-coded into the raw data.

### 7.3 Recency baseline

The brief requires the ML model to be benchmarked against a simple
recency-based heuristic ("predict churn if `recency_days` exceeds a
threshold"). The cutoff is chosen on **VALIDATION only** by maximizing F1
over integer recency values observed on TRAIN (`threshold_days = 18` in
the executed run). Ranking metrics use `recency_days` as the score;
Brier / calibration use recency min-max scaled by the TRAIN maximum.
Head-to-head TEST metrics vs XGBoost are in
[`reports/model_evaluation/model_comparison.csv`](reports/model_evaluation/model_comparison.csv).

### 7.4 Modeling approach

XGBoost binary classifier (`src/modeling.py`), sklearn `ColumnTransformer`
fit on TRAIN only (median impute for numerics; most-frequent + one-hot for
categoricals). TRAIN class imbalance is handled with
`scale_pos_weight = n_neg / n_pos`. A modest fixed configuration is used
(`n_estimators=400`, `learning_rate=0.05`, `max_depth=5`, `subsample=0.8`,
`colsample_bytree=0.8`, `reg_lambda=1`, `random_state=42`). The probability
threshold is chosen on VALIDATION by maximizing F1 among cutoffs whose
predicted-positive rate is between 0.5× and 3× validation prevalence
(selected threshold **0.63**). TEST is scored once.

### 7.5 Evaluation metrics

Executed on TEST (`src/evaluation.py`): PR-AUC (primary), ROC-AUC,
precision, recall, F1, Brier score, precision@k / recall@k (top 5/10/20%),
plus PR, ROC, and calibration plots in `reports/model_evaluation/`.

### 7.6 SHAP explainability

Planned: SHAP values computed on the trained model's TEST-split
predictions to produce global feature importance and local
per-customer explanations (`src/explainability.py`,
`notebooks/04_shap_explainability.ipynb`).

### 7.7 Causal / uplift approach

The dataset is designed to support either an uplift-modeling approach or
a matched-cohort comparison, using the natural variation in exposure to
Discount / Loyalty Nudge / Personal Outreach campaigns in
`marketing_touchpoints.csv`. Correlation between intervention exposure
and retention will **not** be treated as proof of causation — pre-match
covariate balance checks are required before any effect is reported
(`src/causal_analysis.py`).

### 7.8 Revenue-at-risk methodology

Once churn probabilities exist: `Revenue at Risk = P(churn) ×
expected_customer_value`, letting the team prioritize high-value,
high-risk customers rather than treating all predicted churners equally
(`src/revenue_roi.py`).

### 7.9 Intervention ROI methodology

Combines the causal intervention-effect estimates (7.7) with revenue-at-risk
(7.8) and intervention cost to rank Discount vs. Loyalty Nudge vs. Personal
Outreach by expected net ROI.

## 8. Reproducibility

Everything is driven by a single master random seed (`MASTER_SEED = 42`
in `src/data_generation.py`). Re-running the pipeline end-to-end from a
clean checkout reproduces byte-identical CSVs.

## 13. Limitations

- All data is synthetic; behavioural relationships are designed to be
  realistic but are ultimately generator assumptions, not observed
  real-world patterns.
- The synthetic `loyalty_tier` field is generated as a fixed customer
  attribute rather than a time-varying one; a production system would
  need to capture tier history for a fully leakage-safe as-of value
  (documented as a caveat in the leakage audit).
- Churn prevalence varies from ~9.6% (latest snapshot) to ~12.6% (test
  split, averaged across snapshots) depending on which point(s) in time
  are sampled — this reflects genuine time-variation in the synthetic
  engagement-decline trend, not measurement error, but should be kept in
  mind when comparing to the brief's single ~9% figure.
